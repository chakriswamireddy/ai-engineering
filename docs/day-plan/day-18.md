# Day 18 — Persistent Memory

## Objective

Day 17 built the store and drew the seam: the three states got a home, episodic memory became recallable receipts (finished task records, served verbatim), and semantic memory — the **distiller** (episodes → concepts), the **relevance-scored recall**, and the **expiry/re-prompt loop** — was named, left `PENDING`, and passed to today without a builder. Today the seam is built. The curriculum is literal: Day 18 is *Persistent Memory*, with the deliverable *basic persistent memory subsystem* and five learn items — *memory retrieval, memory relevance, expiration, deletion, privacy* — and its objective is the decisive phrase *"add controlled long-term memory."* The word "controlled" is the whole day: memory is added the way Day 15 added read-only privilege and Day 17 added a closed store — under provenance, under principal, under expiry, under deletion, and under the standing rule that *persistent memory only if it earns its keep*, which today stops being a pledge and becomes a measurement. The curriculum's four candidate memories are already tagged from Day 17: **previous investigations** → episodic (Day 17 recallable; Day 18's job is to make that recall *relevant* — scored, not most-recent), **known Saleor concepts** and **past successful workflows** → semantic (distilled over episodes, gated by `authored ⊆ grounded`, expired by relevance rather than only by a clock), and **user preferences within the application** → a **data table, not a memory** — the seam applies even to the curriculum's own list. The deliverable is the day's two tranches on Day 17's store: the **relevance-scored recall** (Day 8/9's retrieval turned on the agent's own past — return the finished investigation that *matches*, scored, and cite it as an episode, never promote it into a lesson without the distiller), and the **distiller** (N episodes → one grounded concept, carrying its source episodes, minted only under `authored ⊆ grounded`, and decayed by relevance with the clock as a backstop). The day's invariant is Day 17's **`recalled ⊆ authored`** followed by the distiller's own **`authored ⊆ grounded`**: you may only recall what the system wrote, and the system may only *distill* what its own grounded evidence supports — a distilled claim is a grounded claim, or it is not minted. And the day's cost honesty changes for the first time in the series' memory: **memory is no longer free** — distillation is a chat, recall *can* be a chat, so the meter that Day 17 refused to fake is now the day's headline, with the control (M8) proving that off ≡ Days 12–17.

## Duties Outline

### 1. Learn: Memory Retrieval — Day 8/9's Engine, Turned on the Agent's Own Past (45 min)

- [ ] Learn what "memory retrieval" means once Day 17 exists: the agent's past is a **catalogue of records** (task records, transcripts, checkpoints — all with provenance, all `principal`-keyed), and retrieval is **Day 8/9 applied to that catalogue**: index the agent's own finished work, score it against a new question, and serve the episodes that *match* — not the most recent, not the most verbose. Day 17 built the shelf; Day 18 builds the *search over the shelf*.
- [ ] Name the retrieval surface honestly: episodes are **recalled as episodes** — a finished investigation is a receipt, returned verbatim with its `task_id`, its evidence, its conclusion, and its provenance. Recall does **not** re-word, merge, or generalize; generalization is the *distiller's* job (Day 18's §5), and the two shelves must not blur, or a receipt starts reading as a lesson (S8's boundary, now with a search engine on top).
- [ ] Reuse the series' retrieval arithmetic exactly where it fits: Day 8's `Retriever` (lexicon + fusion), Day 9's `rel()` + MMR are the scoring machinery for *episodes*, not new models — the catalogue of episodes is small by construction (sized, expiring, principal-keyed), so retrieval over it is the *free* deterministic tier, exactly as Day 9 proved on corpora.
- [ ] Pre-commit the honest limit: retrieval returns **memories as retrievable records**, and the *metric* of a recalled episode is "did the new question actually need that past investigation" — which is Day 21's benchmark, not a hope. The day ships the mechanics (M1) and names the judgment.
- [ ] Keep Day 17's closed-store rule across the new read path: recall reads only **`principal`-matching, unexpired, provenance-bearing** records through the same keying; a recall that serves a foreign principal's investigation is Door S8 opened from the retrieval side.

### 2. Learn: Memory Relevance — the Only-if-Useful Meter, Now a Scorer (45 min)

- [ ] Learn Day 18's core learn item as the fulfillment of Day 7's (and Day 17's) pledge: the curriculum's *"introduce persistent memory only if useful"* and *"memory relevance"* are the **same sentence, the second word of it**. Relevance is the meter: Day 17 could only *name* the "only if useful" measurement (S3 load-bearing, S9 invisible-when-off); Day 18 owns the *scorer* — a recalled memory must beat the alternative (a fresh investigation) to be worth recalling, and a memory below threshold is **not served**, it is the RAG gate (Day 7's refusal) on the memory shelf.
- [ ] Learn the two relevance judgments Day 18 must make and keep apart: **episodic relevance** (does this *past investigation* answer this *new question* — score the episode's signature vs the question) and **semantic relevance** (does this *distilled concept* explain this *new question* — score the concept's claim vs the question). Both feed one recall path; both have one refusal rule below threshold.
- [ ] Pre-commit the counterweight Day 17 reserved: a memory that **changes no answer** in a memory-free control is **overspending**, not enrichment. M8 is the fixture: with memory off (or with an empty store), Days 12–17 run exactly as green; on a question a fresh investigation answers well, a recalled memory must *improve* it to justify the harvest it cost — the "only if useful" meter recording a miss is a *good* day.
- [ ] Learn the failure shapes relevance protects against, Day 9's way: **irrelevant recall** (the most recent investigation served because it is recent — C1's closest ancestor), **half-relevant recall** (a concept that matches one term of a two-intent question), and **confidently wrong recall** (a stale concept that *sounds* like the answer — the memory-flavored day's cardinal sin). Each becomes a fixture.
- [ ] Pre-commit the ordering: build retrieval first, distillation second, because **the scorer decides whether a distilled concept is ever worth minting** — distillation's budget is only justified if scored recall of the result beats a fresh investigation often enough to pay for itself.

### 3. Design: The Persistent Memory Subsystem — Two Tranches, One Store (30 min)

- [ ] Design Day 18 as what Day 17 named it: the **distiller** and the **relevance-scored recall** were never new stores — they are *consumers* of Day 17's store. The design rule: **no new storage shape**. Episodic recall reads `kind=episodic` records; the distiller writes `kind=semantic` records into the *same* file-backed store with the *same* schema, provenance, TTL, and deletion; Day 17's guards run untouched.
  ```text
  Day 17 store (unchanged) ──read──▶ relevance scorer ──top-k, above τ──▶ the answer loop
       ▲                                   ▲
       │ write                             │ episodes + supports
   [working/conversation/task]      [the distiller: N episodes → 1 concept]
       ▲                                   │ write (kind=semantic, provenance = episodes)
       │                                   ▼
   the runs (Days 12–17)          Day 17 store (unchanged; expiry/delete/privacy as-is)
  ```
- [ ] Design decision #1 — **episodic recall is a read-path function, not a feature.** `recall(question, principal)` = retrieve(past_investigations_of(principal), question) → top-k above τ → attach each episode's evidence + `task_id` + provenance into the running context as *an episode*, cited like any Day 6 citation. Day 8/9's mechanics, Day 7's gate, Day 17's keying — no new machinery.
- [ ] Design decision #2 — **the distiller is a chat, gated by code.** One batch job (or the day's run) hands N finished task records to one model call under a strict prompt: *distill what the evidence supports, cite the source episodes, never invent.* The output concept is **`{claim, source_episodes[], strength, ttl, created_by, schema}`** and is minted only if: ≥2 source episodes agree (a single episode is a receipt, never a lesson), no source contradicts the claim (`authored ⊆ grounded` over the union), and the claim is *general* — it survives re-wording, and the day asks nothing more of it than Day 14's assertion contract, moved up a shelf.
- [ ] Design decision #3 — **expiration becomes relevance-governed, with the clock as backstop.** Day 17's TTL stays (the hard floor); Day 18 adds the metric — a concept is served only while its **strength** (supporting episodes, recency, recall hit-rate) clears the recall threshold; decay is *re-prompt-driven* (each served recall can refresh `accessed_at`; a concept that stops supporting new episodes decays by relevance, then by clock). Semantic expiry is Day 17's clock made honest (its §6 question answered in code).
- [ ] Design decision #4 — **privacy is a data-table duty, baked in at design time.** *User preferences within the application* are **not a memory**: they are a small `principal`-keyed **data table** — stored facts the agent may cite as data (Day 17 tagged them "a data table, never generalized into 'the user always…'"), readable only by their principal, never distilled, never served as a lesson. The privacy learn item covers the whole subsystem: memory is the agent's most personal surface, so principal-bounded reads, no generalization from personal data, consent-visible provenance, and first-class deletion are **design decisions, not features**.
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's four invariants: **(a)** `recalled ⊆ authored` (Day 17, untouched); **(b)** `authored ⊆ grounded` (the distiller's gate: a distilled claim is grounded in its source episodes or not minted); **(c)** recall is scored and above-τ or absent (relevance is the meter, never recency or vibes); **(d)** memory off ≡ Days 12–17 (M8 — over-memory is a bug, Day 17's S9 reflex on the memory shelf).

### 4. Build: Episodic Recall — Relevant Past Investigations, Scored (1 hour)

- [ ] Build the **episodic index**: at run-end, the finished task record becomes `kind=episodic` with a compact **signature** (question paraphrase, tools used, evidencing entities/order ids, the conclusion) — enough for lexical scoring, small enough to keep the shelf sized. `recall()` = Day 8's `Retriever` over the signatures of the principal's episodes, Day 9's dedup/MMR to drop near-duplicate investigations, Day 7's threshold gate, top-k enforced by the context budget (Day 6's rule).
- [ ] Serve the episode **as an episode**: the recalled record enters the answer path with its `task_id`, its evidence, its conclusion, and its provenance — a block the answer may *cite*, never a block the answer may absorb as asserted fact; `cited ⊆ retrieved` (Day 6) holds over *episode records* the same way it held over *chunks*. M1 proves the scoring: the *matching* investigation returns above the non-matching one.
- [ ] Route by shelf, not by enthusiasm (M7): a recalled incident that reveals a pattern ("order 4 and order 9 both failed at checkout with payment-declined") is served **as two episodes** — it is *not* promoted inline to "checkout failures trace to payments"; that promotion is the distiller's, and only it may mint the concept. The answer loop never generalizes; it cites episodes and lets the distiller (or the user) draw the lesson.
- [ ] Keep the retrieval half free and deterministic: the episode catalogue is bounded, expiring, and locally indexed — `recall()` is lexicon + fusion + MMR over stored signatures, the exact arithmetic Day 8/9 verified, costed at **0 chats, 0 embeds** on the read path. The `PENDING` seam from Day 9 (a real cross-encoder relevance scorer) stays `PENDING`; its chat meter, if ever paid, is recorded, not assumed.
- [ ] Prove the seam with hostile fixtures: an **empty** episode shelf → `recall()` returns nothing (not a hallucinated "past" — the M8 empty-store control); a **stale** episode (expired on Day 17's clock) → acts absent (Day 17's S5, enforced on the recall path too).

### 5. Build: Semantic Memory — the Distiller, Episodes → Grounded Concepts (1 hour)

- [ ] Build the **distiller** as one guarded chat over Day 17's `kind=episodic` records: batch N finished investigations (N ≥ the sample-size rule), one prompt under the distiller's contract — *distill only what the evidence supports; name each supporting episode; generalize only across episodes that agree; do not mint from a single receipt* — and parse the result into a candidate concept.
- [ ] Enforce **`authored ⊆ grounded`** in code, before write: every claim in the candidate concept must be satisfiable against the **union of its source episodes' evidence** (each episode carries its evidence; the check is set membership over that union — Day 14's `asserted ⊆ returned`, reconstructed for distillation). A claim no source episode actually observed → the concept is **rejected at mint time**, never stored softened; a candidate with **one source episode** → rejected by the sample-size rule (a lesson from one run is an anecdote); a candidate whose claim contradicts any source → rejected (a concept that claims a pattern its own evidence denies is the semantic card of Day 13's fabrication).
- [ ] Mint with full provenance: the concept record is `kind=semantic`, `created_by = {run_id, step, source: "distiller"}`, `source_episodes = [task_ids]`, `strength = |agreeing episodes| / |episodes considered|`, `ttl` from policy. Day 17's store accepts it **because it carries provenance** — the distiller is just another authorized writer, and S7's write-time check is its tab-keep.
- [ ] Close the loop the curriculum implies: a **past successful workflow** is the semantic form of a *verified* investigation (a plan that ran green to `conclusion ⊆ evidence`), and a **known Saleor concept** is a concept whose claims the doc corpus (Days 6–10) corroborates. Both are distilled the *same* way; the difference is the source set, and the day records which source class each concept came from — so "known Saleor concepts" can never be minted from a single customer's checkout story.
- [ ] Keep the honest `PENDING`: the *routing* of the distiller (when it fires, the sample-size gate, the groundedness check, provenance minting) is `MECH` — deterministic functions of stored episodes. Whether a **real model distills a concept that is actually correct, general, and useful** is `PENDING` until a real gateway run — Day 21's benchmark on Day 18's distiller, exactly as Day 21 owns the planner's quality.

### 6. Build: Semantic Expiry, Deletion, and the Re-prompt Loop (45 min)

- [ ] Make **expiration the curriculum's learner, not Day 17's leftover**: Day 17 proved the *clock* (`expires_at` consulted first, compaction). Day 18 adds the *meter* — each `recall()` records `accessed_at`; each successful re-serving refreshes it; a concept whose `strength` falls below its recall threshold (support decayed, episodes expired, or recall stops matching) is **demoted below τ — alive but unserved**, and then the clock — the backstop, not the policy — retires it. M4 proves the ordering: a concept that decays in *relevance* acts as absent *before* its TTL bites (Day 17's honest question answered in code).
- [ ] Wire the **re-prompt loop** the day owns: when a served concept stops earning its keep (unmatched recall × N turns, or a *contradiction* — a fresh investigation's grounded evidence now denies the concept), the loop either (a) **re-distills** the concept over the larger episode set, (b) **decays** it below threshold, or (c) **retires** it (delete). A concept is never *refreshed by re-serving alone* — recency is not truth (Day 9's lesson, on the memory shelf).
- [ ] Keep **deletion Day 17's** and extend its reach to the distiller: `forget(principal, key)` removes a record *and its derived aggregates* — for semantic memory the derived aggregate is **every concept that cites the record as a source** (a concept whose supporting episode was deleted is re-grounded: re-checked against its remaining sources, and if it now fails the sample-size rule, retired too). M5 proves end-to-end unreachability. The honest limit stands, named loudly: a concept **already recalled and absorbed into a past answer** cannot be un-remembered from that answer — "you cannot un-distill" is the day's most honest boundary, and deletion means *future* recall, not retrospective erasure. That limit is Day 18's privacy footnote, not a hidden failure.
- [ ] Re-run Day 17's retention fixtures on the new kind: expired concepts act absent, compaction reclaims, deletion is visible in the trace — S5/S6 were written for shelves; M4/M5 re-assert them for the distiller's shelf, so Day 18 adds a *consumer*, not a retention loophole.

### 7. Build: Privacy — Memory Is Personal Data, Preferences Are a Table (45 min)

- [ ] Learn the curriculum's privacy item as Day 18's **first-class data duty**: memory is the agent's most personal surface — it holds what users asked, what the agent investigated about them, and their stated preferences. The day's stance: **every memory is personal data of a principal**, recall is principal-bounded (Day 17's `principal` keying extended to *every* read, including the distiller's batch source selection), and nothing leaves a principal's shelf without that principal's shelf's own rules.
- [ ] Build **user preferences as a data table, not a memory** (Day 17's routing, now coded): a `kind=preference` record per principal — `{principal, key:"currency", value:"EUR", source:"user-stated", created_at, ttl}` — served to *its own* answer loop as **cited data**, never distilled, never generalized ("the user always pays in EUR"), never merged across principals. M6 proves a cross-principal preference read returns **nothing**.
- [ ] Enforce the distiller's privacy wall: distillation runs over **task records the runs authored**, and a task record that *contains personal data* (an order, a customer id — Day 14/15's data surface) is **seeded only as provenance, never as lesson material**: the distiller may form "checkout failure → inspect payment" from order data; it may **not** form "customer X always fails" — personal identifiers do not survive distillation. A concept that names a principal is a **privacy failure**, rejected by the mint-time check.
- [ ] Respect deletion as a *right*: `forget(principal, key)` on a preference or a memory is the same first-class, aggregates-included operation (M5); day's-end we can show a principal *everything* the subsystem holds about them (`list_for(principal, *)` — Day 17's inspectability is Day 18's privacy answer) and *nothing* it can recall about another.
- [ ] Record the tension honestly, Day 15's way (privileges beat parsers, the data role was the guarantee): **privacy is enforced by the write path and the keying, not by the prompt.** The distiller is a model; its *output* is gated by code (groundedness, sample-size, no-principal-names), exactly as Day 15 gated the SQL by role, not by asking nicely.

### 8. Test: The Memory Set (M1…M9) (1 hour)

- [ ] Pre-register the memory fixtures (**M1…M9** — *memory* fixtures, one per property, plus a control) with expectations written **before** any run:
  - **M1** episodic recall relevance: two finished investigations (one matching the new question, one not) → `recall()` serves the **matching** one above the other, scored — not "most recent", not "only one found". (Day 8/9's C1 lesson, on the memory shelf.)
  - **M2** distiller: N ≥ 2 episodes sharing a pattern → one concept `{claim, source_episodes, strength, ttl, created_by}` minted with **provenance**, citing its sources.
  - **M3** `authored ⊆ grounded`: a candidate concept claiming what its source evidence does **not** contain → **rejected at mint time**; a **one-episode** candidate → rejected (a receipt is not a lesson); a candidate contradicting a source → rejected.
  - **M4** semantic expiry: a concept decays in relevance (support expired) → **acts absent** on recall *before* its clock TTL; the clock (Day 17) is the backstop; hostile tiny-strength config proves the demotion fires.
  - **M5** deletion end-to-end: `forget` on an episode removes **every concept that cites it** (re-grounded or retired); the record is **unreachable everywhere**; deletion visible in the trace.
  - **M6** privacy: a cross-principal preference or memory read → **nothing returned**; user preferences serve as **data**, never distilled; a concept naming a principal → **rejected**.
  - **M7** shelf boundary: a recalled episode is served **as an episode** (cited, not absorbed); no run-to-run bleed except through the distiller's minted concept — the S8 boundary, now with a search engine on top.
  - **M8** **control**: memory **off** (or an empty store) → Days 12–17 run *unchanged and exactly as green* (A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9) — over-memory, memory that alters a memory-free answer, is a bug.
  - **M9** curriculum routing: the four potential memories land on their real shelves — previous investigations → **episodic**; known Saleor concepts + past successful workflows → **semantic** (distilled); user preferences → **data table** — and the "only if useful" measurement is recorded on the two answerable rows (does recall beat a fresh investigation? yes/no, with wall_ms and cost).
- [ ] Split the day honestly, Day 7's way: **M1, M3's checks, M4, M5, M6, M7, M8, and M9's routing are `MECH`** — deterministic functions of the scorer, the sample-size/groundedness gate, the expiry/backstop math, `forget`, keying, and stored payloads: offline, free, reproducible. **M2's and M3's *real-model* distillation quality** (does a real model distill a concept that is correct, general, and grounded) and **M9's real "useful?" verdict** on a real question are `PENDING` → Day 21's benchmark on Day 18's distiller and recall. The stub proves the **subsystem**; a real key proves the **memory**.
- [ ] Re-run Days 7–17's fixtures as the no-regression gate **with memory on and off**: M8 guarantees the *off* case equals Days 12–17 exactly, and the *on* case with an empty store must also equal it (a memory subsystem that changes a memory-free answer is an M8 failure); the distiller and the recall path change **no** stored record's schema — Day 17's store and its S1–S9 tests run untouched.
- [ ] Apply Day 7's isolation, one owner per symptom: **irrelevant recall** → the scorer/τ owns it; **a concept that over-claims** → the distiller's `authored ⊆ grounded` gate owns it; **a stale concept recalled** → semantic expiry owns it; **a deleted episode's concept survives** → `forget`'s derived-aggregate removal owns it; **a cross-principal read** → the keying owns it; a **memory that changed a memory-free answer** → the M8 control owns it. One owner per symptom, in the trace.

## Deliverable

The **basic persistent memory subsystem** — Day 17's store given its purpose, under Day 17's guards, with the day's two invariants:

```text
a finished run  ──episodic record──▶  [kind=episodic, provenance, TTL]        (Day 17 shelf)
new question ──recall()──▶ Day 8/9 scorer over the principal's episodes
    │                          ↳ top-k, above τ, cited AS AN EPISODE        (M1, M7)
    └─ no match ─▶ fresh investigation (Days 12–17)        ──"only if useful" measured (M9)
N matching episodes ──distiller (1 chat, gated by code)──▶  [kind=semantic concept]
    │                        ↳ sample-size ≥ 2     → a receipt is not a lesson
    │                        ↳ authored ⊆ grounded → rejects over-claim, contradiction, 1-source
    │                        ↳ no principal names  → privacy is a mint check
    ▼
concept {claim, source_episodes[], strength, ttl, created_by}
    ↳ served only above τ (relevance is the meter, the clock is the backstop)   (M4)
    ↳ forget() re-grounds every concept that cites its source                  (M5)
user preferences ──▶ a principal-keyed DATA TABLE — cited as data, never distilled (M6)
   invariants:  recalled ⊆ authored   (Day 17, untouched)
                authored ⊆ grounded   (Day 18's headline: a distilled claim is grounded or not minted)
   OFF switch:  memory off ≡ Days 12–17                                       (M8 control)
```

Day 17 answered "what survives the run, who may read it, and how does it die." Day 18 answers **"what is the past *for*."** A new question consults the agent's own past and gets the *matching* investigation back, scored and cited as the episode it is; episodes that share a pattern are distilled into a grounded lesson — a known workflow, a known concept — minted only under `authored ⊆ grounded`, decayed by relevance with the clock as a backstop, deletable with its aggregates, and invisible across principals; user preferences stay *data*; and the curriculum's "only if useful" stops being a pledge and becomes a number in the table — with the control proving, the way S9 proved for state, that a memory which changes a memory-free answer is a bug, not an upgrade.
