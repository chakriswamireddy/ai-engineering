# Day 21 — RAG Evaluation

## Objective

Twenty days built a machine that reads (Days 6–10), acts (11–12), survives failure (13), computes (14–15), plans (16), remembers (17–18), decides its own architecture (19), and — since Day 20 — judges its own work. Day 20 built the court and made the month's promise mechanically true: **"stop judging AI quality manually,"** delivered as a golden dataset, a harness, a deterministic judge, and a regression gate, with the semantic cells — citation correctness, groundedness, answer relevance — parked at exactly one door: **Day 21's.** The curriculum now says what sits behind that door in two words: **"measure retrieval."** Its metrics list is the four the series has been hand-computing or hand-wishing since Day 7 — **Recall@K, Precision@K, MRR, retrieval relevance** — and its "also evaluate" line is the semantic tier Day 20 refused to fake: **citation correctness, groundedness, answer relevance.** Its deliverable is the thing Days 7–20 kept promising and never delivered as a machine: **the RAG benchmark.** Two of the four retrieval metrics are already *in code* before the day begins (`ai_os/trace.py`'s `expected_stats` has computed recall@K and MRR since Day 7); Day 21's genuine problems are the two that cannot be computed from a ranking alone — **precision@K**, which needs a notion of *relevant* that only a pre-registered golden set can supply, and **retrieval relevance**, which needs a pointwise judgment the free tier has approximated since Day 9 and the paid tier has refused to buy since Day 9 §7's standing `PENDING` — and then the semantic cells that can only be filled *against a real run*, never by a model's voice and never by a judge that re-answers.

Day 21's first principle is the honest census: **the benchmark inherits exactly the assertions Day 20 refused to fake** — `judged ⊆ pre-registered` (a verdict only where the pre-registered expectation supports it, hash-pinned), the judge reads the trace and never re-answers, and the harness off ≡ days-without. To that Day 21 adds its own spine: **the two metrics nobody ever wrote down are the ones the golden set finally makes writable.** Recall@K and MRR fell out of `expected_stats()` because *expected* was pre-registered as early as Day 7's YAML; Precision@K never existed because *relevant* was never constrained — the set of docs that may legitimately fill a retrieved slot, as opposed to the required set that must. Day 21 writes `relevant_also` into the golden rows (a new, optional, oracle-disciplined set: docs known to be on-topic that need not be retrieved), giving precision a home on the answerable rows and an honest `n/a` on the rows with no defensible relevance set (E-family — nothing is relevant; judge-hunting F/G/H — relevance is the trap). A metric that *refuses to be computed* where its definition is empty is more honest than a metric that pretends. The second principle: **the semantic tier Day 20 parked is claim-coverage, not impression.** Day 20's `claims[] + must_not_assert[]` was built, oracle-derivable, and waiting for a grader; Day 21 grades it as exactly the apprentice rules demand — the answer is judged by which pre-registered claims it asserted (recall of truth) and which forbidden assertions it made (a `must_not` hit is a coverage failure of class "forbidden," the worst kind). Groundedness is the same machine turned around: every asserted claim must trace to the context the run actually showed, and a claim with no line of evidence in that context is a hallucination by definition. And the day's counterweight, in the series' oldest voice: **the RAG benchmark is itself a fixture with a control — R9** because a bench that improves answers is an answer-path change wearing a lab coat, and the only honest number a benchmark may add to a run it reproduces byte-for-byte is zero.

## Duties Outline

### 1. Learn: The Loaded Objective — "Measure Retrieval" and the Four Metrics (45 min)

- [ ] Parse the day's two-word objective as discipline, not mood: **"measure retrieval"** bans the sentence the series has survived twenty days without being able to challenge — "it seems better" (Rule 4's banned phrase) — but aimed at the retriever specifically, and it bans it *structurally*: the day is not a report on one run, it is a **benchmark**, i.e. a fixed set of rows, a fixed set of metrics, a pinned baseline, and a reproducibility contract, so Days 22–30 can ask "did retrieval improve?" and receive a number, not a re-read table. Day 9's reports were hand-written tables; Day 7's were hand-read tables; the whole point of Day 20 was to make the *judgment* re-runnable — and the whole point of Day 21 is to make the *retrieval verdict* a machine's output on a fixed ship.
- [ ] Inventory which of the four curriculum metrics the series already owns, because the day's build is smaller than it looks: **Recall@K and MRR have existed since Day 7** — `ai_os/trace.py`'s `expected_stats()` computes `recall_at_k` and `mrr` from the pre-registered `expected` sets, and Day 7's §4 decision tree is literally a recall classifier (missing-from-top-K → `missing-retrieval`); **Precision@K has never existed** — the metric nobody could compute because *relevant* was never constrained, and precision computed against an empty relevance notion is a proportion of noise, not a score; **retrieval relevance** exists twice: as Day 9's free `rel()` arithmetic (the heading/rare/cosine pointwise signal in `ai_os/rerank.py`) and as the Day-9§7 `PENDING` it refuses to buy — `relevance_cross_encoder` still raises `NotImplementedError` to stay unbilled. Day 21's census of the four: **two baked (recall@K, MRR), one defined-but-free (retrieval relevance), one genuinely empty (precision@K)** — and the empty one is the day's new field.
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's split, unchanged from every day since Day 7: **the rank-and-membership metrics are `MECH`** — recall@K, MRR, and (with a pre-registered `relevant_also` set) precision@K are arithmetic over the trace, zero chats, byte-reproducible; **the meaning metrics are the semantic tier** — citation correctness, groundedness, answer relevance — exactly the cells Day 20's `judge.py` layer-2 stamped `PENDING: owner Day 21`. The day does not blur that line; it fills one side and stamps the other with owners.
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's three invariants before any build: **(a) `judged ⊆ pre-registered`** carried from Day 20, extended to the new field — a `relevant_also` set, when present, is written *before* any run and graded under the same content hash (a relevance set widened after the run to flatter precision is the golden-analog of the curve fit Day 20's V1 exists to catch); **(b) the judge must not re-answer** — the semantic cells grade the trace's own context and the run's own answer, never a fresh generation; **(c) R9** — bench off ≡ days-without, Day 20's V9 reflex on the benchmark surface.

### 2. Learn: Recall@K, Precision@K, MRR — the Rank That Means Something, and the One That Doesn't (45 min)

- [ ] Learn **Recall@K** as the series already owns it: the fraction of pre-registered expected docs that reached the top-K — `expected ⊆ retrieved`, per row and per family. Day 7's nine, Day 10's code six, and Day 20's golden rows all pre-registered their `expected`; the bench turns the row tables into a number and then into a *row-level verdict with one owner each*. The real-store floor is already written: Day 9's real-corpus table shows 7 of 9 doc rows **leaking** at top-8 (best expected docs at fused ranks 16–30+), and the bench's first act is re-running those very rows and debiting the leaks to retrieval by name (per Day 9 §7's own re-attribution rule).
- [ ] Learn **MRR** as the series has reported it since Day 8 — the reciprocal of the first expected doc's rank (the `best_rank` column of `expected_stats`), ΣΔMRR as the improvement metric, "leaks" as the anti-regression guard. The bench inherits the discipline unmodified: an improvement is `ΣΔMRR > 0` *with row-level leaks = 0*, exactly Day 9's sweep contract — the 0-leak guard is not optional, it is the difference between "better" and "different."
- [ ] Learn **Precision@K** as the day's genuine new thing, and why it is the one metric nobody hand-collected first: precision asks "of the K slots, how many are *relevant*?" — and *relevant* is a judgment the ranking cannot make, so hand-grading had to either skip it (which every day did) or invent it per row (which would have been manual judgment, the exact thing Day 20 banned). The series' answer, in one sentence: **the golden set is the relevance oracle.** Each answerable golden row gains a third pre-registered set — `relevant_also`: the docs known to be on-topic that are not required — D1's twin family, B1/B2's sibling lifecycle pages, C2's surrounding attribute-file pages — and `precision@K = |retrieved ∩ (expected ∪ relevant_also)| / K`, computed by membership like recall, `MECH`, free. Where no defensible relevance set exists, the cell is **`n/a` by construction**: E-family (nothing is relevant) and judge-hunting F/G/H (relevance is the trap) — and an `n/a` is a measurement choice, not a gap.
- [ ] Learn why the pair matters as a pair, with the series' oldest example as its own row: **D1 — "How do I complete a checkout?"** — was pre-registered in Day 7 as *duplicated context — 4 of 5 slots are near-dupes of one meaning*. On the stub-era mechanics, D1's recall@K is 1.0 (the how-to page is retrieved) and its MRR is 1.0 (it ranks first) — and both metrics say the row is fine while the row's own pre-registered hypothesis says it is polluted. **Precision@K is the first metric that sees the disease D has been pre-registering since Day 7**: a recall- and MRR-perfect row with precision 0.2 is not a pass; it is the day's proof that the two legacy metrics are load-bearing but blind on exactly the seam D built to expose.
- [ ] Pre-commit the honest edge-cases: **empty expected** (E-family) → recall@K and MRR are *undefined*, `n/a`, never 0.0 (0.0 would grade "refused to retrieve anything, pass," a category error); **K vs budget_chars** → the trace's `k_from_budget` makes top-K a budget artifact, so recall@K/precision@K are comparable only at the same K — the bench pins `k` per corpus (doc ~9–15, code symbol-units) exactly as the days already did.

### 3. Learn: The Semantic Tier Day 20 Parked — Citation Correctness, Groundedness, Answer Relevance (45 min)

- [ ] Learn **citation correctness** in its two halves, the first carried from Day 6 and never relaxed: *mechanically*, `cited ⊆ retrieved` — every citation points at a doc that was actually retrieved (fabrication caught by set membership — Day 6's oldest invariant, V3b's G-row, now a column); *in meaning*, the *support* half — the cited doc actually supports the claim it is cited for (a lifecycle page that is in-context but irrelevant to a claim about apps passes membership and fails support). That support half is the cell Day 20 parked and the K-row's whole point: **a citation can be real and wrong.**
- [ ] Learn **groundedness** as *claims ⊆ evidence*: every claim in the answer traces to the context the run actually showed — Day 16's `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, made per-claim instead of per-conclusion. The trace already records the shown context; groundedness is the run's answer re-read against *that exact context*, never against a fresh retrieval. The rule is the day's sharpest: **an answer whose claim has no line of evidence in the shown context is a hallucination by definition, independent of how fluent or confident the prose reads** (V3a's F-row, now with a metric to fail).
- [ ] Learn **answer relevance** as **claim-coverage** — the bridge Day 20's Notes asked about out loud and Day 21 must answer: *does `expected_answer.claims[] + must_not_assert[]` expand into a claim-coverage metric?* The day's answer: **yes — and it is the honest bridge from the deterministic floor to the semantic tier, because a pre-registered claim is a checkable fact, not a vibe.** Coverage = the fraction of pre-registered `claims[]` the answer asserted; an answer asserting a `must_not_assert` is a coverage failure of class *forbidden*, the worst kind. The deterministic subnet covers the claims that are *deducible* — contained or derivable-by-normalization from the evidence — and the residue (paraphrase, inference, meaning-only support) is the flagged lane: bounded, budgeted, **one call per unresolved claim, never re-answering the question**.
- [ ] Pre-commit the semantic judge's budget and its rule, Day 20's intact: **deterministic first, the paid lane off by default (`EVAL_JUDGE=deterministic`), and the machine may only *decide* where membership can** — an undecidable claim is *recorded with an owner*, never silently passed, because a semantic verdict that cannot re-run is Day 20's drift problem wearing a fancier hat.

- [ ] Design decision #1 — **two of the four metrics exist; precision and relevance are the build**: recall@K/MRR columns come from `trace.expected_stats` unchanged; **precision@K** needs `relevant_also` minted into the golden YAML under the same oracle-discipline as `expected` (pre-registered from corpus structure, graded under the day's hash); **retrieval relevance** reuses Day 9's free `rel()` signal as its default measure and shelves the cross-encoder behind a flag that *remains `NotImplementedError` until the free column shows a leak the free signal misses* — the Day-9§7 pricing question ("does paying for semantics beat the free rel() arithmetic?") finally answered by a bench rather than promised.
- [ ] Design decision #2 — **the semantic judge grades the run's own evidence, never a fresh answer**: groundedness and claim-coverage read the context the trace actually built and the answer the run actually gave; the only billable cell is a claim the deterministic subnet cannot trace, budgeted at 1 chat per unresolved claim, flagged, off by default, and never allowed to flip a MECH verdict (Day 20's `deterministic_verdict` rule, carried verbatim).

- [ ] Design decision #4 — **the LOM verdict gets its real answer here**: Day 9's lost-in-the-middle protocol proves the *ordering* knob works (`MECH`); whether slot 1 beats slot 3 for the model's correctness is the semantic question the bench is now equipped to grade — the R7 answer-relevance column across a `best_first` sweep on the real store, closing the oldest open `PENDING` in the retrieval line.
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's negatives with owners, not wishes: **real model tool choice** (Day 11's `PENDING`) is Day 22's agent benchmark; **observability of the graded pipeline** is Day 23's trace; **judge poisoning** stays Day 25's seam; **the fully-golden prose answer** stays named, not built — the claims form is the replacement, and claim-coverage is what fills it out.

### 6. Build: The Semantic Judge — Citation Correctness, Groundedness, Answer Relevance (1 hour)

- [ ] Build `ai_os/semantic_judge.py` as layer 2 of Day 20's `judge.py`, filling the three parked cells with claim-coverage as the room's single discipline: **citation support** (does the cited doc state the claim's key terms — an evidence-containment probe by default, the flagged lane as fallback), **groundedness** (every claim contained-in or derivable-from the shown context), **answer relevance** (coverage of `claims[]`, and a `must_not_assert[]` hit is *always* a fail — a forbidden assertion is not a coverage miss, it is a category error).
- [ ] Define the deterministic subnet precisely, so the `MECH`/`PENDING` line never blurs: a claim is **deducible** if, after normalization (stopword-fold, case-fold, term-Jaccard against the shown context — Day 9's `text_sim`, reused), it is contained or its key terms co-occur in the evidence the answer could have cited — decided by arithmetic, byte-reproducible (the same trace graded twice is the same verdict). A claim that is *not* deducible is **undecided**: recorded, owned, optionally explained by the flagged lane, never silently passed.
- [ ] Wire the flagged lane behind `EVAL_JUDGE` (default `deterministic`): a bounded, budgeted call per undecided claim, writing to the `semantic:` column with `graded_by: model`, never flipping a `deterministic_verdict`, carrying the drift disclaimer (Day 20's V4, recalibrated for per-claim calls). - [ ] Run the judge through its hostile rows *before* the happy ones, Rule 3's way: the **F-row** (fluent but wrong) must fail groundedness, the **G-row** (fabricated citation) must fail `cited ⊆ retrieved` at Day 6's invariant, the **H-row** (citation-present, answer-unsupported) must fail coverage, the **K-row** must fail citation *support* while passing `cited ⊆ retrieved` — the proof that the semantic tier catches what membership alone cannot, and the demonstration that no amount of prose buys a pass.

## Deliverable

The **RAG benchmark** — Day 20's court rendered into a machine that measures retrieval and grades the answer's relationship to the evidence, on the golden set and against pinned baselines:

```text
   THE GOLDEN SET (five fields + relevant_also; hash-pinned; E-family refusals,
     judge-hunting F/G/H, new adversarial I1/J1/K1) — written, and pinned, before any run
        │
        ▼
   THE R-BENCH — one place that asks, per row, on a chosen store:
     R1 recall@K     — expected ⊆ top-K                        (MECH — Day 7's field)
     R2 precision@K  — (expected ∪ relevant_also) ∩ top-K / K  (MECH — the day's new field)
     R3 MRR          — first expected doc's rank                (MECH — Day 8's field)
     R4 relevance    — mean rel() over kept context, vs the priced cross-encoder lane (OFF)
     R5 citations    — cited ⊆ retrieved · and the cited doc supports the claim
     R6 groundedness — every claim traces to the shown context
     R7 relevance    — claim coverage; a must_not_assert is always a fail
     R8 refusal      — τ calibrated on real answerable-vs-E scores; the gate-flip decision
     R9 control      — bench off ≡ days-without; the bench doesn't fix answers
        │
        ▼
   THE VERDICT ROWS + the pinned baseline → Days 22–30 ask "did it improve?" and get a number
```
