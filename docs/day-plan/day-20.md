# Day 20 — Evaluation Fundamentals

## Objective

Nineteen days built a machine that deserves to be judged, and the curriculum finally says it in two words: **"Stop judging AI quality manually."** The single-line objective is not a gentle suggestion — it is the read the series has been waiting for, because everything before Day 20 *has* been manual. Day 7's nine pre-registered questions got graded by hand; Day 8/9/10's MRR and recall numbers were gathered question by question; Day 12–19's fixture tables (A, E, Q, T, X, P, S, M, G) were verified by `MECH` checks that needed a human to *build* each one. Day 20 stops that. Its learn items are the four pillars the whole month has been gesturing at since Day 7's `PENDING` column — **golden datasets, regression testing, deterministic evaluation, LLM-as-judge limitations** — and its deliverable is the thing every day since Day 7 has promised Day 20 would arrive with: **the first evaluation harness**. The curriculum's build list is the exact contract: an evaluation dataset containing **question, expected evidence, expected answer, expected tools, failure category** — five fields that are not new paperwork but an inventory of what the series already pre-registered: the question (Day 7's `questions.yaml`, Day 10's `code_questions.yaml`), the expected evidence (Day 7's doc-id sets), the expected answer (the *one* field the series refused to hand-write, and the day's genuine problem), the expected tools (Day 11's registry, Day 19's roles), and the failure category (Day 7's verdict tree, Day 13's `failure_kind` taxonomy). Day 20 builds the **golden dataset** that fills all five fields pre-run, the **harness** that presents each question to production exactly as a user would, the **judge** that grades the *trace* (never re-answers), the **regression gate** that holds Days 7–19 exactly as green while improvement happens, and the **honest lane for LLM-as-judge** — a slot the day refuses to fill by default.

The day's first principle, stated up front and never dropped: **an evaluation harness is a router and a judge, not a grader-by-hand — and the judge's first rule is that it must not re-answer the question.** Every honest number the series has kept lives in the trace (`ai_os/trace.py`: retrieved top-K, expected-set membership, recall@K, MRR, citations, ledger); Day 20's judge reads *that*, not a fresh generation. The second principle is the day's terminus of a discipline running since Day 7: **pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting** (the `questions.yaml` header says it in so many words), so Day 20 makes pre-registration *mechanical* — the golden dataset's five fields are written before any run, the harness refuses to grade an entry whose expectations were patched post-run, and the day's headline invariant is the four-word coronation of that rule: **`judged ⊆ pre-registered`** — a verdict is only issued where the pre-registered expectation supports it; nothing is graded on surprise, on fluency, or on facts the expectation set never contained. And the third principle is the counterweight the series has enforced since Day 16's P9, Day 17's S9, Day 18's M8, and Day 19's G9: **the judge is not a fixer.** An evaluation harness that changes a single production answer — a judge in the answer path, a golden row that leaked into the corpus, a graded run that ran hotter than production — is a bug, measured as a no-change row (V9) the same way over-planning, over-persistence, over-memory, and over-orchestration were. Day 20 hands Day 21 (the RAG benchmark) the judge over retrieval, Day 22 (the agent benchmark) the judge over behavior, and Day 23 (observability) a pipeline it can trace; today it builds the court they all stand in, at a cost of **0 chats** — because the judge's default is deterministic, and the semantic cells it cannot deterministically touch stay `PENDING`, exactly where the series has parked the truth since Day 7.

## Duties Outline

### 1. Learn: The Loaded Objective — What "Stop Judging Manually" Actually Bans (45 min)

- [ ] Parse the curriculum's two-word objective as a discipline, not a mood: **"Stop judging AI quality manually"** bans *manual* judgment — the unrepeatable, unweighted, single-reader grade — not judgment itself. Rule 4 is the apprenticeship's standing command (*how much better? by which metric? on which dataset? at what cost? with what latency? what failures remain?*), and Day 20's insight is that a manual answer to Rule 4 is a *vibe with extra steps*: the metric is right and the dataset is right, but the grade came from one human eyeball and cannot be re-run. Day 20 builds the machine that re-runs it.
- [ ] Learn the four learn items as the pillars they are: **golden datasets** (a fixed, pre-registered set of questions with expected evidence, answer, tools, and failure category — the "battleship" every run is tested against), **regression testing** (Rule 2's ratchet: an improvement must not roll back an already-green behavior — the series' no-regression gate since Day 8's "same questions keep green," now first-class), **deterministic evaluation** (grades computed by set-membership and arithmetic over the trace: exact, single-run, reproducible, free), and **LLM-as-judge limitations** (a stochastic grader grading a stochastic grader is not ground truth — the day names why, and refuses to let it be the default).
- [ ] Inventory how much of the five-field dataset already exists, because the day's *build* is smaller than it looks: **question** → `ai_os/questions.yaml` (the A–E nine, pre-registered from corpus structure) and `ai_os/code_questions.yaml` (CA1–CE1), prefilled with `expected`, `must_not`, `must_refuse`, `predicted`, and `fix`; **expected evidence** → those `expected` doc-id/module-symbol sets; **expected tools** → Day 11's `ai_os/tools.py` registry (search_docs / search_code / get_product / order / customer / run SQL, git, run) and Day 19's role mapping; **failure category** → Day 7's verdict tree and Day 13's `failure_kind` taxonomy. The genuinely *empty* field is **expected answer** — the one the series deliberately refused to hand-write — and Day 20's second-slot problem is to fill it *pre-run* without writing a model's voice.
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's ruling vocabulary: **golden entry** = one row of the five-field dataset; **harness** = the router that presents each golden question to the production path (env-switchable: stub tracer / real gateway / memory on-off / Day 19 topology A-B up to whatever is implemented) and returns the run's trace and ledger unchanged; **judge** = the layered grader that reads traces; **regression gate** = the replay of all prior fixture families that must stay exactly as green; **`PENDING`** = a semantic verdict the deterministic layer cannot support, owned by Day 21 (or 22 for agent behavior).
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's three invariants before any build: **(a) `judged ⊆ pre-registered`** — the judge issues only verdicts the pre-registered expectation supports, and a golden row is graded under the immutable hash of its pre-run registration; **(b) the judge must not re-answer** — it grades the trace, never a fresh generation, or the run it "judges" is not the run it was handed; **(c) harness off ≡ days-without** (V9) — the evaluator is additive and invisible, the P9/S9/M8/G9 reflex on the measurement surface.

### 2. Learn: Golden Datasets and the Pre-Registration Line (45 min)

- [ ] Learn what a **golden dataset** is *for* in this series: not a test-suite dump, but a **hypothesis ledger**. Every entry carries its `predicted` class and its `fix` owner (as Day 7's YAML already does) so a failing row *says which layer's hypothesis died* — the dataset is a set of bets against the system, and each run reports which bets the system upheld, which it lost, and who owns the loss. That is Rule 4's "what failure cases remain" given a home.
- [ ] Learn the golden entry's five fields as the curriculum ordered them, and the dependency between them: **question** (the natural-language user input, verbatim), **expected evidence** (the doc_ids / module#Symbols the corpus must surface — Day 7's law: pre-registered from the corpus *structure*, never from retrieval output), **expected answer** (the *minimal* set of claims the answer must assert, and a `must_not_assert` set it must not — written as facts, not prose, and forced by the oracle: an expected answer that cannot be derived from the expected evidence is a **malformed entry**, rejected by the validator), **expected tools** (the tool or role the correct answer needs — from Day 11's registry and Day 19's routing table), and **failure category** (the expected failure shape if this entry is supposed to fail: out-of-corpus → refusal, ambiguous → wrong-entity, etc., from Day 7's tree and Day 13's taxonomy).
- [ ] Learn the discipline that keeps the golden set honest: it must be **small, deliberate, and adversarial** (Rule 3 — every entry is a kind of thing that has failed before, deliberately rebuilt), it must include the **E-family** (entries whose correct answer is *refusal* — the expected answer field literally says "refuse, and say why"), and it must include **judge-hunting entries** (rows built to defeat the grader itself — a fluent wrong answer, a right answer with a fabricated citation, a right answer that cites correctly but concludes wrongly) so the judge's own failure modes are exercised the same way Rule 3 exercises the system.
- [ ] Pre-commit the anti-curve-fit enforcement: the golden YAML is the commitment, and the harness **grades under the hash of the pre-run registration** — a row patched after the run file exists (an `expected_evidence` edited because the run came up short, an `expected_answer` typed after seeing the output) fails the integrity check (V1), not the run. Day 7's "pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting" becomes a code path.

### 3. Learn: Regression, Determinism, and the Honest Limits of LLM-as-Judge (45 min)

- [ ] Learn **regression testing** as Rule 2's ratchet made mechanical: the series' improvements have already been held to "the same questions stay green" (Day 8's nine, Day 9's `ΣΔMRR` with 0 leaks, Day 10's code 6/6 + doc nine 9/9); Day 20 promotes that discipline into **`tools/regress_all.py`** — a single replay of the full fixture families (A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9, M1…M9, G1…G9, plus the doc/code golden rows) that must come back *exactly as green*. Improvement is measured on the golden set; *preservation* is measured on the regression gate; one without the other is not a Rule-4 result.
- [ ] Learn **deterministic evaluation** as the day's default judge: every check that is set-membership or arithmetic over the trace — `expected ⊆ retrieved` (Day 7), `cited ⊆ retrieved` (Day 6), `conclusion ⊆ evidence` (Day 16), tool-selection equality (`tool == expected_tools`), refusal correctness (E-family), failure-kind classification, cost and latency from the ledger (`ai_os/ledger.py`) — is **MECH**: exact, reproducible run-to-run, single-run (no sampling), and free (0 chats). Deterministic evaluation is not a lesser tier; it is the *only* tier that can prove its own verdict, and the day's report is built on it.
- [ ] Learn **LLM-as-judge limitations** as the day's constraining honesty — the judge's failure shapes, each a fixture: **(a) judge fabrication** — a model grader asserts "the answer omitted X" where X is not a pre-registered expectation nor in the answer's evidence (grading on facts the expectation set never contained → violates `judged ⊆ pre-registered`); **(b) fluency bias** — a confident, well-structured wrong answer out-grades a shy correct one (the judge rewards prose, not grounding); **(c) drift** — the same answer graded differently across runs or models (a stochastic grader cannot issue a reproducible verdict); **(d) collusion/poisoning** — an answer trained to satisfy the grader rather than the user (Day 25's security seam, named today); **(e) cost** — one judge call per semantic grade is a per-question bill the free deterministic tier already answers. Rule: **the LLM judge is a flagged, budgeted, non-authoritative lane** — it may *explain* a verdict, never *decide* one; a semantic verdict it offers is recorded in a `semantic:` column that is `PENDING` by default and re-graded by Day 21.
- [ ] Pre-commit the ordering, Day 7's way: **dataset → integrity → harness → deterministic judge → regression gate → (flagged) model judge last.** You cannot grade an answer the harness did not run; you cannot trust a grade the golden set did not pre-register; you cannot trust the harness until V9 proves it is invisible; and only then is there a trace honest enough for a model judge to *comment on* rather than decide.

### 4. Design: The First Evaluation Harness — One Router, One Judge, the Pre-Registered Set (30 min)

- [ ] Design Day 20 as exactly what Day 19's "court" promised to become (the A/B harness formalized into a benchmark): the harness is a **router**, not a new engine — it presents each golden question to the production answer path (Day 12's loop, Day 16's planner, Day 18's memory, Day 19's topology, all env-switchable to whatever depth is implemented today) and returns the run's trace and ledger *unchanged*:
  ```text
                 the golden dataset (five fields, pre-registered, hash-pinned)
                                      │
                     ┌────────────────┴─────────────────┐
                     ▼                                  ▼
        THE HARNESS (router, not a grader)       THE REGRESSION GATE (prior days)
        presents each question to production,    replays A…M+G+doc/code fixture
        captures the trace + ledger verbatim     families; must stay exactly green
                     │                                  │
                     └────────────────┬─────────────────┘
                                      ▼
                      THE JUDGE — layering, built to not re-answer:
        layer 1  deterministic (MECH, 0 chats): expected ⊆ retrieved
                 · cited ⊆ retrieved · conclusion ⊆ evidence
                 · tool == expected_tools · must_not · refusal == expected
                 · failure == category · cost · latency
        layer 2  semantic — a SLOT, PENDING by default (Day 21/22)
        layer 3  LLM-as-judge — flagged, budgeted, explains but never decides
                                      ▼
                          verdict rows → the eval report → the benchmark Day 21/22 run
   ```
- [ ] Design decision #1 — **the judge grades the trace, never a fresh answer**: `ai_os/trace.py` already records everything a verdict needs (retrieved top-K, expected-set membership, recall@K, MRR, citations, gate decision, ledger, termination), and the judge reads *that*. A judge that re-runs the model is a second production call that must not exist — it doubles the bill, re-bakes sampling noise into a "grade," and grades a run the trace never saw.
- [ ] Design decision #2 — **the judge is layered, with determinism first and semantic verdicts parked**: layer 1 is the full set of set-membership/arithmetic checks (MECH, 0 chats, owned by the day and by `judge.py`); layer 2 is the semantic *slot* — citation *correctness* beyond membership, answer *relevance*, groundedness in meaning — declared `PENDING` with its owner (Day 21's RAG benchmark; Day 22 for behavior); layer 3 is the model judge, off by default (`EVAL_JUDGE=deterministic`), and its output never flips a layer-1 verdict.
- [ ] Design decision #3 — **edges that need rules before the build**: an out-of-corpus entry's expected answer is *"refuse, and say why"* (the E-family as a golden row, not an exception); a question the golden set expects to need two docs but the current system routes to one tool is a *routing row* owned by Day 19's router (reuse the owner map, don't re-litigate it); a cost row is per-query from the ledger, and the day's report column shows tokens and latency *because Rule 4 demands them*, not because Day 20 is a benchmark yet (that is Day 21/22's).
- [ ] Pre-commit the day's counterweight, the series' invariant in its measurement form: **the harness is not in the answer path** — V9 is the fixture where the evaluator, installed and unused, changes nothing (byte-identical answers and fixtures), the same no-change row P9/S9/M8/G9 enforced at their surfaces. An evaluation harness that improves the answer is an answer-path change wearing a lab coat.

### 5. Build: The Golden Dataset — Five Fields, Every Row Pre-Registered (1 hour)

- [ ] Build `ai_os/eval_dataset.py` + `ai_os/golden.yaml`: the five-field schema with a **validator**, reusing the series' readers — convert `questions.yaml` and `code_questions.yaml` verbatim into golden rows, then enforce the three new-field rules: expected answer is a **`claims[]` + `must_not_assert[]`** structure written as minimal facts (never a model-voice paragraph), **oracle-derivable** (`ai_os/oracle.py` already substitutes perfect evidence; run it *offline* to check the expected answer is reachable from the expected evidence, or reject the entry as malformed), and expected tools must exist in the Day 11 registry / Day 19 role table (or the entry is rejected).
- [ ] Seed the golden set deliberately: the doc nine (A–E) and the code fixtures (CA1–CE1) become rows *as-is* (their `predicted`/`fix` fields ride along so a failing row still names its owner), plus the day's own **judge-hunting** additions — F: "fluent but wrong" (right citations, wrong conclusion — feeds the `conclusion ⊆ evidence` check), G: "hallucination under confidence" (a fabricated citation the deterministic layer must catch), H: "citation-present, answer-unsupported" (cites correctly but asserts beyond evidence) — so V3/V4 can prove the judge cannot be fooled or drifted.
- [ ] Pin the pre-registration mechanically: each golden row gets a `registered_at` + the dataset's **content hash**; the harness refuses to grade under a stale hash (a patched `expected_evidence` or a post-run `expected_answer` is a V1 integrity failure before a single run). Day 7's line between a report and curve-fitting becomes `assert hash == pinned_hash`.
- [ ] Fill the expected-tools field from the routing truth the series already owns: a doc question routes to `search_saleor_docs`, a code question to `search_saleor_code`, a data question to the schema-grounded SQL tool (Day 15's role), a multi-step checkout investigation to the Day 16 plan's tool set — so "expected tools" is the *role-context router* (Day 19) written down per row, making tool-selection a checkable column instead of a `PENDING` shrug.
- [ ] Keep the free tier free: the dataset is YAML plus one validator run — **0 chats, 0 embeds** — and every entry's `expected_answer.claims[]` is derived from its expected evidence, so no golden row ever requires a paid call to author.

### 6. Build: The Harness and the Judge — Grade the Trace, Never Re-Answer (1 hour)

- [ ] Build `tools/eval_harness.py`: the router — load the pinned golden set, run each question through the production path exactly as a user would (env-switchable harness: `--mode stub|real`, memory on/off, topology A/B, embedding provider), and collect `{question, trace, ledger, config}` per row *without touching the answer path's code*. The harness respects Day 4's ledger economics, Day 13's timeout wall, and Day 19's receipts when the topology is B — it presents, it does not interfere.
- [ ] Build `ai_os/judge.py` as the layered grader: layer 1's deterministic checks per family — `expected ⊆ retrieved` (recall@K, MRR — the Day 8/9 metrics, now produced *by the judge*, not by hand), `cited ⊆ retrieved` (Day 6's invariant), `conclusion ⊆ evidence` (Day 16's), `tool == expected_tools`, `must_not` avoidance, E-family refusal correctness, and failure-category match (Day 7's tree, Day 13's `failure_kind`); layer 2's semantic slots (`citation_correctness`, `answer_relevance`, `groundedness`) left `PENDING` with owners; layer 3 the optional model judge behind `EVAL_JUDGE`. The judge's output is a **verdict row** — `pass | fail | pending`, with the failing checks named and **one owner per symptom** (Day 7's isolation map, reused: evidence miss → retrieval; citation leak → generation/Day 20 itself; wrong tool → routing; refusal miss → gate/τ).
- [ ] Build `tools/regress_all.py`: one command replays the full fixture families — A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9, M1…M9, G1…G9, plus the doc/code golden rows — reusing `ai_os/fixtures.py`'s `run_fixtures` and the `test_dayN.py` checks into a single gate whose report is "every row exactly as previously green, or the diff, with owner." This is Rule 2's ratchet as a command.
- [ ] Record which answers it is *not* grading (the day's negatives, owned not wished): answer *relevance* and citation *correctness* in meaning are **Day 21's** RAG benchmark; agent behavior (steps, tool-selection recall@1, unnecessary calls, failure recovery, cost, latency) is **Day 22's**; observability of the graded pipeline is **Day 23's** — Day 20 builds the judge that *parks* those verdicts, and parking is a feature, not a stall.

### 7. Build: The LLM-as-Judge Lane, and Its Honest Limits (45 min)

- [ ] Wire the slot, not the verdict: `JudgeKind = deterministic | model | none` behind `EVAL_JUDGE` (default `deterministic`), and the model lane — one flagged, budgeted judge call per semantic cell, writing to the `semantic:` column — is **wired and off**. The deterministic layer's verdict row is complete without it; the model lane is an optional *comment*, and its rows carry `graded_by: model|provider` plus the drift disclaimer (V4's numbers).
- [ ] Encode the day's rule in code: an LLM judge may **explain** a `pending` (say *why* the semantic cell is unresolved, optionally), it may never **decide** one — the `deterministic_verdict` field is only ever set by layer 1, and a "model flips a pass to fail" is a `judged ⊆ pre-registered` violation the harness flags red (V4).
- [ ] Build the judge-corruption fixtures so the *judge* is broken deliberately, Rule 3's way: **V3a** a fluent wrong answer (correct citations, wrong conclusion) must fail `conclusion ⊆ evidence` at layer 1; **V3b** a right answer with one fabricated citation must fail `cited ⊆ retrieved`; **V3c** an out-of-corpus confident answer must fail the E-family refusal check — and the same rows run through the model lane show why a stochastic grader *can't* be trusted with them (its verdict drifts; V4 records the drift).
- [ ] Keep the budget honest: the model lane bills **1 chat per graded semantic cell**, is never invoked by default, and its per-row cost sits in the report next to the deterministic row's **0 chats** — the day's whole economics, Rule 4's "at what cost" answered by construction until Day 21 decides whether paying for semantics is worth it (the standing cross-encoder question, now on the evaluation shelf).

### 8. Test: The Evaluation Set (V1…V9) and the Counterweight (1 hour)

- [ ] Pre-register the evaluation fixtures (**V1…V9** — *verdict* fixtures: the properties of grading itself, several of them hostile, plus the control) with expectations written **before** the harness runs:
  - **V1** golden dataset integrity: every row passes the five-field validator (claims derivable from expected evidence via the oracle; expected tools registered; failure category in the taxonomy), and a **patched row** (post-run `expected_evidence` / `expected_answer`) is rejected by the hash before grading (pre-registration is the line, in code).
  - **V2** judge reproducibility: the *same trace* graded twice → **byte-identical verdict rows** (the judge has no hidden sampling; a nondeterministic layer-1 check is a bug in the judge, not a property of answers).
  - **V3** the judge cannot be fooled by fluency: the fluent-wrong, fabricated-citation, and citation-present-answer-unsupported rows (**judge-hunting** F/G/H) each fail their deterministic check → a fluent paragraph does not buy a pass, and a correct-looking answer cannot hide an ungrounded assertion.
  - **V4** judge honesty under drift: the same answer run through the model lane twice → identical deterministic verdict; the `semantic:` column may differ, and the difference is **recorded** as evidence the stochastic tier cannot decide (its own fixture).
  - **V5** regression gate: `tools/regress_all.py` replays A…M+G plus doc/code golden rows → **exactly as green** as the prior days (a day's improvement is measured on the golden set; *preservation* is measured here; one without the other is not a Rule-4 result).
  - **V6** golden-set coverage: the doc nine, code fixtures, the judge-hunting rows, and the E-family refusals are all present with all five fields → the dataset deliberately contains *kinds of failure*, not happy paths (Rule 3 at the dataset level).
  - **V7** measurement, not manual: the eval report for the whole golden set is generated with **zero human screentime** — humans write expectations *before*, the machine writes verdicts *after*, and the report is shipped, not adjudicated by eyeball.
  - **V8** pre-registration before runs: the dataset is authored and **hash-pinned before any run file exists**; the harness runs in fixed order (expectations → integrity → runs → verdicts) and a run that started before its row's registration is flagged (the anti-curve-fit fixture).
  - **V9** **control**: the harness installed and unused changes **nothing** — a golden run's trace equals the same question's production trace (byte-identical), and no fixture row moves; the judge is not a fixer, P9/S9/M8/G9 measured on the measurement surface (Rule 6's economy, applied to evaluation itself).
- [ ] Split the day honestly, Day 7's way: **V1, V2, V3, V4's deterministic lane, V5, V6, V7, V8, V9 are all `MECH`** — deterministic functions of the dataset, the registration hash, the traces, and the ledger: offline, free, reproducible, and the whole of the day's payload. **The `PENDING` cells are the semantic ones**: citation correctness in meaning, answer relevance, groundedness beyond set membership — all **Day 21's** RAG benchmark; agent-level behavior verdicts **Day 22's**; and the model judge's own `semantic:` column stays `PENDING` by construction, because a graded semantic cell is exactly what Day 21 will grade *against a real run*, not what a flagged optional lane decides today.
- [ ] Re-run the no-regression gate the day's own way: Days 7–19 through `regress_all.py` with the harness off and on — the **off** pass is the baseline (the evaluator adds nothing), the **on** pass replays the golden set's own rows and must agree with the production traces (V9), and any row that a run cannot reproduce is a **harness bug to fix**, never a fixture to weaken.
- [ ] Apply Day 7's isolation, one owner per symptom: **an expected doc missing from top-K** → retrieval owns it (the Day 8/9/10 backlog, now a verdict column with an owner); **a citation not in retrieved** → generation, and Day 20's judge is the first thing to *say* it without a human; **tool ≠ expected_tools** → routing (Day 19's role table); **refusal missed** → gate/τ calibration, parked at Day 21; **a verdict changed by re-running the judge** → a judge bug, owned by V2; **a verdict changed by installing the harness at all** → the V9 offense, owned by the harness.
