# Day 20 Notes — Evaluation Fundamentals

> Notes answering `docs/day-plan/day-20.md`.
> Nineteen days built a machine that *reads* (Days 6–10), *acts* (Day 11), *drives itself* (Day 12), *survives failure* (Day 13), *computes* (Day 14), *defends itself* (Day 15), *thinks before acting* (Day 16), *remembers what it did* (Day 17), *carries a lesson — only if the lesson earns it* (Day 18), and *decides its own architecture* (Day 19). Every one of those days was judged **by hand**: a human built the fixture, read the table, and said "green." The curriculum now says the month's standing practice is the thing to stop doing, in two words: **"stop judging AI quality manually."** Its learn items are the four pillars the series has been gesturing at since Day 7's `PENDING` column — **golden datasets, regression testing, deterministic evaluation, LLM-as-judge limitations** — and its deliverable is the thing days 7 through 19 kept promising a future day would build: **the first evaluation harness**, fed by an evaluation dataset containing the five fields *question, expected evidence, expected answer, expected tools, failure category*. Day 20 formalizes what Day 7 did for nine questions by hand into a machine that does it for the whole series, at 0 chat cost, without a single human eyeball grading the output.
> The single idea: **judgment is not banned — *manual* judgment is; so Day 20 builds a judge that grades the trace, never re-answers, and only ever outruns the expectations that were written before the run.** The golden dataset is Day 7's hypothesis ledger made first-class, with the five curriculum fields and the one the series deliberately refused to hand-write — *expected answer* — reduced to a minimal, oracle-derivable `claims[] + must_not_assert[]`, so no golden row is authored by a model's voice. The judge defaults to **deterministic** (set-membership and arithmetic over the trace: `expected ⊆ retrieved`, `cited ⊆ retrieved`, `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, tool-selection equality, refusal correctness, cost, latency) and parks the semantic cells the deterministic layer cannot touch as `PENDING` with an owner (Day 21 RAG, Day 22 agent); the **LLM-as-judge** lane is wired, budgeted, and **off by default**, because a stochastic grader grading a stochastic answer fails reproducibility, falls for fluency, can be poisoned, and costs a chat where the free tier already decided. The day's invariants: **`judged ⊆ pre-registered`** — a verdict only where the pre-registration supports it, enforced as a hash pin — and **V9's control** — harness off ≡ days-without, the P9/S9/M8/G9 reflex on the measurement surface, because a judge is not a fixer. Day 20 ends with the court standing: the golden set, the harness, the deterministic judge, the regression gate, and a `PENDING` semantic column that Day 21 will fill *against a real run* — which is the whole point of building a judge today.
>
> **The one line the day would die defending: `judged ⊆ pre-registered` — an evaluation harness may only issue a verdict the pre-registered expectation supports, and the moment it grades on what it wishes had been expected, it has started judging manually again.**

## 1. The Loaded Question — "Stop Judging AI Quality Manually"

The curriculum's Day 20 objective is the smallest sentence of the month and, arguably, the one everything else was aiming at: **stop judging AI quality manually.** Read it the way the apprenticeship reads every adjective (Rule 4's discipline): it does not say "stop judging AI." It says judgment is owed — *how much better, by which metric, on which dataset, at what cost, with what latency, what failures remain* — but the judge has been a human eyeball for nineteen days, and a human eyeball is the one grader that cannot be re-run. A manual grade is unrepeatable (the reader's attention moved), unweighted (one reader, one reading), and unavailable to the system as a contract (nothing in the repo knows what "good" was supposed to mean). Day 20 is where "good" gets written down *before* the run, in five fields, in YAML, in code.

The four learn items are the pillars:

- **Golden datasets** — a fixed, pre-registered set of questions, each carrying its expected evidence, expected answer, expected tools, and failure category. This is the "battleship": every run is tested against the same ship, and an improvement is only real if it shows up as a diff on that ship. The series has half of this already, and the day's honesty is to admit it: Day 7's `questions.yaml` is *literally* a golden dataset missing two fields.
- **Regression testing** — Rule 2's ratchet, made mechanical. The series never allowed an improvement to roll back an already-green behavior (Day 8's "same questions keep green", Day 9's `ΣΔMRR` with 0 leaks, Day 10's code 6/6 + doc nine 9/9). Day 20 promotes that habit into `regress_all.py`: one command replays every prior fixture family and demands *exactly as green*.
- **Deterministic evaluation** — grades computed by set-membership and arithmetic over the trace: run-to-run identical, single-run (no sampling), free, and *self-proving*: a deterministic verdict carries its own reproducibility in its method. This is not a lesser tier; it is the only tier that can justify every pass it prints.
- **LLM-as-judge limitations** — the constraining admission. A stochastic model grading a stochastic answer cannot issue a reproducible verdict, is fluent-bait (a confident wrong answer out-grades a shy correct one), drifts across runs and providers, can be trained against (the poisoning seam), and costs a chat per cell the free tier already answered. Day 20 names all five and refuses to let a model judge *decide*; at most it *explains*.

The pre-registration line is the day's spine, and the series wrote it down long before Day 20 — the `questions.yaml` header: *"expected sources written BEFORE running; pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting."* Day 7 hand-built nine questions that stand in front of every later improvement; Day 8 said "the same questions keep green" and meant regression; Day 9 ran the same table in a new engine and *called it evaluation*; Day 10 did it on two corpora. Day 20 is the point where that habit stops being a habit and becomes a subsystem: the golden dataset is the commitment, the hash pin is the enforcement, and `judged ⊆ pre-registered` is the invariant that says a verdict is only ever as good as the expectation it was written against.

## 2. Golden Datasets and the Pre-Registration Line

A golden dataset in this series is **not a test-suite dump — it is a hypothesis ledger.** Look at what Day 7 already stores per question: `predicted` (the hypothesis) and `fix` (the layer that owns the failure if the hypothesis survives). A golden entry is a *bet against the system*: it says "this question needs these docs, this answer, these tools, and if it fails here is the layer that owns the loss." Running the golden set is scoring the bets; a failing row does not just say *no* — it says *which layer's hypothesis died* (Day 7's verdict tree, Day 13's `failure_kind`, Day 19's owner map). That is Rule 4's "what failure cases remain" with a home and an owner.

The five fields, in the curriculum's order, with the dependency that makes them a schema:

1. **Question** — the natural-language user input, verbatim. Already in `questions.yaml` and `code_questions.yaml`.
2. **Expected evidence** — the doc_ids / `module#Symbol` sets the corpus must surface. Day 7's law applies unchanged: pre-registered from the corpus *structure*, never from retrieval output. Already in the YAML as `expected`.
3. **Expected answer** — the field the series deliberately **refused to hand-write**, and Day 20's genuine problem. The answer to it is *reduction*: an expected answer is not a model-voice paragraph, because nobody should author what the system should say; it is **`claims[] + must_not_assert[]`** — the minimal set of facts the answer must assert, and the assertions it must avoid. And it is **oracle-derivable**: Day 7's `oracle.py` already substitutes perfect evidence; run it *offline* and the expected answer must be reachable from the expected evidence, or the entry is **malformed** and the validator rejects it. A golden row whose "expected answer" cannot be derived from its expected evidence is not an answer, it is a hope.
4. **Expected tools** — the tool or role the correct answer needs, from Day 11's `tools.py` registry and Day 19's role-context table. Filling this field is how "the agent used the wrong tool" stops being an argument and becomes a checkable column.
5. **Failure category** — the expected failure shape *if* this entry is supposed to fail: out-of-corpus → refusal, ambiguous → wrong-entity, near-duplicate → lost-in-the-middle, etc. — the taxonomy Day 7's tree and Day 13's guards made.

The disciplines that keep the set honest are three. **It must be small, deliberate, and adversarial** (Rule 3: every entry is a *kind of thing that has failed before*, deliberately rebuilt — the nine are that already: obvious control, multi-document seam, ambiguous terminology, similar documents, out-of-corpus). **It must contain its own E-family** — entries whose expected answer is *"refuse, and say why"* — because a golden set without a refusal row would grade a system that invents answers. **It must contain judge-hunting rows** — entries built to defeat the grader itself: a fluent wrong answer (right citations, wrong conclusion), a right answer with a fabricated citation, an out-of-corpus answer with confident prose — so the judge's own failure modes get the Rule-3 treatment too, and the day can prove it cannot be fooled by fluency or drifted by re-runs.

The pre-registration enforcement deserves its own sentence because it is the whole day in one mechanism: the golden YAML is the commitment, each row carries `registered_at`, the dataset carries a **content hash**, and the harness refuses to grade under a stale hash. A row whose `expected_evidence` is patched after the run came up short, or whose `expected_answer` is typed after seeing the output, fails **V1** — the integrity check — before a single verdict exists. Day 7's one-line contract becomes a code path, and curve-fitting stops being a temptation and starts being an exception to catch.

## 3. Regression, Determinism, and the Honest Limits of LLM-as-Judge

**Regression testing** is Rule 2's ratchet put in one command. The series has never allowed "better" to mean "different": Day 8 shipped a retriever that had to keep the seven green rows green while fixing C1; Day 9's whole report was a per-row delta with a "0 leaks" guard; Day 10 ran *two corpora* and demanded the doc nine stay 9/9. Day 20 names what that has always been — **regression testing** — and builds `regress_all.py`: a replay of the fixture families (A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9, M1…M9, G1…G9, plus the doc/code golden rows) that must come back *exactly as green*. The split is deliberate and must not blur: **improvement is measured on the golden set; preservation is measured on the regression gate.** One without the other is not a Rule-4 result — an improvement that rolls back a green row is a regression, and a regression-free change that moves no golden row is a rearranged deck, not an improvement.

**Deterministic evaluation** is the day's default judge, and the operative word is *default*. Every check that is set-membership or arithmetic over the trace is graded by code: `expected ⊆ retrieved` and its recall@K/MRR derivatives (Day 7/8/9's metrics, now produced by the judge rather than hand-collected), `cited ⊆ retrieved` (Day 6's invariant, standing since the first answer), `conclusion ⊆ evidence` (Day 16's), `tool == expected_tools`, `must_not` avoidance, E-family refusal correctness, failure-category match, cost and latency from the ledger (Day 4's `ledger.py`). These are **MECH**: exact, reproducible, single-run, free (0 chats), and — the property that matters — *self-justifying*: the grade's method is the proof of the grade. Determinism is not the limit of honest evaluation; it is the bedrock under whatever semantic floor the series eventually pays for.

**LLM-as-judge limitations** are the constraining half of the day, and the day refuses to hand-wave them. Five failure shapes, each with a fixture's worth of weight:

1. **Judge fabrication** — the model grader asserts "the answer omitted X," where X is neither a pre-registered expectation nor in the answer's evidence. That is a grade on a fact the expectation set never contained — a *violation of `judged ⊆ pre-registered` wearing a model's confidence*.
2. **Fluency bias** — a confident, well-structured wrong answer out-grades a shy correct one. A model judge admires prose; the deterministic judge reads membership. V3 exists to show the difference in one table.
3. **Drift** — the same answer graded differently across runs or providers. A stochastic grader cannot issue a reproducible verdict, and a non-reproducible verdict cannot be the basis of a benchmark. V4 records the drift as the lane's own indictment.
4. **Collusion / poisoning** — an answer tuned to satisfy the grader rather than the user. This is Day 25's security seam, named today with its owner, because the moment the judge becomes the goal, the judge has been suborned.
5. **Cost** — one model-judge call per semantic cell is a per-question bill where the free deterministic tier already answered. Rule 4's "at what cost" settles it before the model lane is ever switched on.

The day's rule, encoded in `judge.py`: **an LLM judge may *explain* a `pending`, it may never *decide* one.** The `deterministic_verdict` field is only ever set by layer 1; the model lane writes to a `semantic:` column that is `PENDING` by default, budgeted, flagged `graded_by: model`, and re-graded by Day 21 against a real run. Ordering follows Day 7's discipline: dataset → integrity → harness → deterministic judge → regression gate → model judge *last*, because there is no trace honest enough for a model to comment on until the deterministic court has proven the trace honest and invisible.

## 4. The First Evaluation Harness — One Router, One Judge, the Pre-Registered Set

Day 19 built a court for the multi-agent question and called it a harness that "can present any question to any topology and score the answer against pre-registered expectations." Day 20 is the formalization Day 19's notes named forward: *"the exact machinery Day 20 formalizes into a benchmark."* So the design is deliberately unsurprising — a **router**, not a new engine:

```text
                  the golden dataset (five fields, pre-registered, hash-pinned)
                                      │
                     ┌────────────────┴─────────────────┐
                     ▼                                  ▼
        THE HARNESS (router, not a grader)       THE REGRESSION GATE
        presents each question to production,    replays A…M+G + doc/code
        captures trace + ledger verbatim         families; exactly as green
                     │                                  │
                     └────────────────┬─────────────────┘
                                      ▼
                       THE JUDGE — built to not re-answer:
        layer 1  deterministic (MECH, 0 chats): expected ⊆ retrieved ·
                 cited ⊆ retrieved · conclusion ⊆ evidence
                 · tool == expected_tools · must_not · refusal == expected
                 · failure == category · cost · latency
        layer 2  semantic — a SLOT, PENDING by default (Day 21 / Day 22)
        layer 3  LLM-as-judge — flagged, budgeted, OFF; explains, never decides
                                      ▼
               verdict rows → the eval report → Day 21/22's benchmark inputs
```

Decision 1 — *the judge grades the trace, never a fresh answer.* The trace (`ai_os/trace.py`) already carries everything a verdict needs: retrieved top-K, expected-set membership, recall@K, MRR, duplicate ratio, gate decision, citations, fabrication flag, ledger. A judge that re-answers is a second production call: it doubles the bill, re-bakes sampling noise into the "grade," and — worst — grades a run the trace never saw. "The run that was judged" must be identical to the run that happened, or the verdict is about an answer nobody gave.

Decision 2 — *the judge is layered, determinism first, semantics parked.* Layer 1 is the full set of set-membership/arithmetic checks, all `MECH`, all owned by `judge.py`. Layer 2 is the semantic *slot* — citation correctness beyond membership, answer relevance, groundedness in meaning — declared `PENDING` with owners (Day 21's RAG benchmark; Day 22 for behavior). Layer 3 is the optional model lane, off by default. The layering is not subtlety; it is the series' whole `MECH`/`PENDING` split (which has been Day 7's exact partition for thirteen days) applied to the *judge itself*.

Decision 3 — *edges get rules before the build.* An out-of-corpus entry's expected answer is *"refuse, and say why"* — the E-family becomes a golden row, not an exception. A question that needs two docs but routes to one tool is a **routing row** owned by Day 19's role table, re-litigated nowhere. Cost and latency appear in the report *because Rule 4 demands them*, not because Day 20 is the benchmark yet — Day 21/22 are the benchmark; today ships the report that feeds them.

And the pre-committed counterweight, in the series' own voice: the harness is **not in the answer path**. V9 is the fixture where the evaluator, installed and unused, changes nothing — a golden run's trace equals the same question's production trace byte-for-byte, and no prior fixture row moves. Over-evaluation is a bug measured the way over-planning (P9), over-persistence (S9), over-memory (M8), and over-orchestration (G9) were measured: as a no-change row. **The judge is not a fixer.**

## 5. Build: The Golden Dataset

`ai_os/eval_dataset.py` + `ai_os/golden.yaml` — the five-field schema with a validator that *reuses* the series' readers: `questions.yaml` and `code_questions.yaml` convert verbatim into golden rows (their `predicted`/`fix` fields ride along, so a failing row still names its owner), and the three new rules are enforced in code:

1. **Expected answer is `claims[] + must_not_assert[]`** — minimal facts, never a model-voice paragraph. Authoring the system's voice by hand is exactly the manual judgment the day bans, in its most seductive form; the claim list is the patient, repeatable version.
2. **Oracle-derivability** — `oracle.py` substitutes perfect evidence offline; if the expected answer is not reachable from the expected evidence, the entry is *rejected as malformed*. This single rule keeps the golden set honest at the seam where most evaluation datasets quietly rot (expectations written from the hope of the answer, not the structure of the evidence).
3. **Expected tools must exist** — in the Day 11 registry / Day 19 role table, or the entry is rejected. The `expected_tools` field is, in effect, Day 19's role-context router written down per row: a doc question expects `search_saleor_docs`, a code question `search_saleor_code`, a data question the schema-grounded SQL role, a checkout investigation the Day 16 plan's tool set. Tool-selection stops being a `PENDING` shrug and becomes a column.

The seed set is deliberate: the doc nine (A–E) and code fixtures (CA1–CE1) verbatim, plus the day's own **judge-hunting** additions — F (fluent but wrong: right citations, wrong conclusion — feeding the `conclusion ⊆ evidence` check), G (hallucination under confidence: a fabricated citation layer 1 must catch), H (citation-present, answer-unsupported: cites correctly, asserts beyond evidence). These three are the golden set's adversarial joint, the same way Rule 3 deliberately broke happy paths in Days 11–13.

Pre-registration is pinned mechanically: `registered_at` per row, a **content hash** for the dataset, and "refuse to grade under a stale hash" in the harness. Day 7's "pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting" stops being a comment in a header and becomes an `assert hash == pinned_hash`. And the whole dataset is authored at **0 chats, 0 embeds** — every `claims[]` derived from its expected evidence — so the golden set costs the ledger nothing to exist.

## 6. Build: The Harness and the Judge

`tools/eval_harness.py` is the router, and its discipline is that it must present each golden question to production *exactly as a user would* — environment-switchable (`--mode stub|real`, memory on/off, Day 19's topology A/B, embedding provider) but intervention-free: it returns `{question, trace, ledger, config}` and touches no answer-path code. It respects what the days already built into that path — Day 4's ledger economics, Day 13's timeout wall, Day 19's receipts when topology is B. It presents; it does not interfere.

`ai_os/judge.py` is the layered grader. Layer 1's deterministic checks per family: `expected ⊆ retrieved` with recall@K and MRR (the numbers settle into a verdict, no hand), `cited ⊆ retrieved`, `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, `tool == expected_tools`, `must_not` avoidance, E-family refusal correctness, failure-category match. Layer 2's slots (`citation_correctness`, `answer_relevance`, `groundedness`) are declared `PENDING` with owners. Layer 3 is the optional model judge behind `EVAL_JUDGE`. The judge's output is a **verdict row** — `pass | fail | pending`, the failing checks named, and **one owner per symptom** (Day 7's isolation map, reused): evidence miss → retrieval; citation leak → generation; wrong tool → routing (Day 19); refusal miss → gate/τ (Day 21).

`tools/regress_all.py` is Rule 2 as a command: one replay of the fixture families — A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9, M1…M9, G1…G9, plus the doc/code golden rows — reusing `fixtures.py`'s `run_fixtures` and the `test_dayN.py` checks into a single gate whose output is "every row exactly as previously green, or the diff, with owner." A day's improvement is scored against the golden set; its *innocence* is scored here; anyone who ships one without the other has shipped noise.

The negatives are named with owners, not wished: answer relevance and citation correctness *in meaning* are **Day 21**'s RAG benchmark; agent behavior — steps, tool-selection, unnecessary calls, failure recovery, cost, latency — is **Day 22**'s agent benchmark; observability of the very pipeline being graded is **Day 23**'s trace. Building a judge that *parks* verdicts is the day's most honest feature: the court is exactly as good as the verdicts it will eventually bear, and the semantic verdicts belong to days that can bear them against a real run.

## 7. The LLM-as-Judge Lane, and Its Honest Limits

`JudgeKind = deterministic | model | none` behind `EVAL_JUDGE`, default `deterministic`. The model lane is **wired and off**: one flagged, budgeted judge call per semantic cell, writing to the `semantic:` column with `graded_by: model|provider` and the drift disclaimer (V4's numbers). The deterministic row is complete without it; the model lane is an optional *comment*, never a vote.

The day's rule, in code: `deterministic_verdict` is only ever set by layer 1; a "model flipped a pass to fail" is a `judged ⊆ pre-registered` violation the harness flags red before it can land in the report. The judge-corruption fixtures (V3a/b/c) are built to break the *judge* deliberately, Rule 3's way: the fluent-wrong answer must fail `conclusion ⊆ evidence`; the fabricated citation must fail `cited ⊆ retrieved`; the confident out-of-corpus answer must fail the E-family refusal check. The same rows, run through the model lane, show precisely why a stochastic grader cannot be trusted with them — its verdict drifts (V4 records the drift), and a benchmark built on a drifting grader is a washing machine with a broken spin.

Budget honesty is structural: the model lane bills **1 chat per graded semantic cell**, is never invoked by default, and its per-row cost sits in the report next to the deterministic row's **0 chats**. Rule 4's "at what cost" is answered by the report's columns, not by faith — and the cross-encoder question (Day 9's standing `PENDING`: does paying for semantics beat the free `rel()` arithmetic) now has a judge's hat and a slot in the Day 21 bench where it has always belonged.

## 8. Test: The Evaluation Set (V1…V9) and the Court

Expected behavior written **before** the harness runs, one fixture per property plus the hostile ones and the control:

| ID | What it proves | Input / injection | Expected |

The `MECH`/`PENDING` split is Day 7's, exact as ever. **V1, V2, V3, V4's deterministic lane, V5, V6, V7, V8, V9 are all `MECH`** — deterministic functions of the dataset, the registration hash, the traces, and the ledger: offline, free, reproducible, and the whole of the day's deliberative payload. **The `PENDING` cells are semantic**: citation correctness in meaning, answer relevance, groundedness beyond set membership — **Day 21's** RAG benchmark; agent-level behavior verdicts — **Day 22's**; and the model judge's own `semantic:` column stays `PENDING` by construction, because a graded semantic cell is exactly what Day 21 will grade against a real run, not what a flagged optional lane decides today. The stub proves the **court**; a real key proves the **verdicts** — the series' oldest sentence, now about the judge itself.

The no-regression line runs the day's own way: Days 7–19 through `regress_all.py` with the harness **off** (the baseline: the evaluator adds nothing) and **on** (the golden set's own rows must agree with production traces, V9). Any row a run cannot reproduce is a **harness bug to fix**, never a fixture to weaken — the same refusal Day 19 made for a topology that failed where the monolith succeeded. And isolation stays one-owner-per-symptom: an expected doc missing from top-K → retrieval (the Day 8/9/10 backlog, now a verdict column); a citation outside retrieved → generation, and *the judge says it first without a human*; `tool != expected_tools` → routing (Day 19's table); a missed refusal → gate/τ, parked at Day 21; a verdict that changes when the judge re-runs → a judge bug, owned by V2; a verdict that changes because the harness was installed at all → the V9 offense, owned by the harness.

## 11. Daily Learning Record

### Problem

Nineteen days produced a system that is capable, honest, grounded, planned, remembered, and architecturally decided — and every single verdict about it has been issued by a human reading a table. The curriculum's Day 20 objective is the smallest sentence of the month: **"stop judging AI quality manually."** The problem is not that the series lacks evaluation — it is that evaluation has *worked* by hand for nineteen days (Day 7's nine questions and their pre-registered expected sets, Day 8/9/10's MRR and recall collected question by question, Days 12–19's fixture tables run and eyeballed), and that manual grading is precisely the unrepeatable, unweighted, single-reader kind the apprentice rules keep forbidding without a name for it (Rule 4 asks *how much better, by which metric, on which dataset, at what cost* — and then a human answers it by reading a report). The genuine difficulty: **the golden dataset's middle field, *expected answer*, is the one the series deliberately refused to hand-write** — authoring the model's voice by hand is the seductive form of manual judgment — and the day must fill all five curriculum fields pre-run, build a judge that reads traces rather than re-answering, hold the whole prior series green while it does, and build a lane for LLM-as-judge that is honest about *why* it cannot be the default. The deliverable is the **first evaluation harness** — and the discipline is that judgment must become repeatable before it is trusted to be anywhere near right.

### Concept

**An evaluation harness is a router and a judge — and the judge's cardinal rule is that it must not re-answer the question.** The golden dataset is a **hypothesis ledger**, not a test-suite dump: five fields per row (question, expected evidence, expected answer, expected tools, failure category), all written before any run and pinned by a content hash, with the expected answer forced into a minimal, **oracle-derivable `claims[] + must_not_assert[]`** so no row is authored by a model's voice and no row's expectation can be patched after the run (Day 7's "pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting" becomes `assert hash == pinned_hash`). The judge is **layered**: layer 1 is deterministic — set-membership and arithmetic over the trace (`expected ⊆ retrieved`, `cited ⊆ retrieved`, `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, `tool == expected_tools`, refusal correctness, failure category, cost, latency) — reproducible, free, and self-proving; layer 2 is a **semantic slot** declared `PENDING` with owners (Day 21 RAG, Day 22 agent); layer 3 is the **LLM-as-judge lane**, wired, budgeted, and **off by default**, because a stochastic grader fails reproducibility, falls for fluency, drifts across runs, can be poisoned (Day 25's seam), and costs a chat where the free tier already decided. The day's invariants: **`judged ⊆ pre-registered`** — a verdict only where the pre-registration supports it, enforced as a hash pin — and **V9's control** — harness off ≡ days-without, a judge that is not a fixer, the P9/S9/M8/G9 reflex on the measurement surface. Everything is `MECH` and costs **0 chats**; the only bill is the optional model lane, and the semantic truth the day cannot grade is parked exactly where the series has always parked it: at Day 21's door, against a real run.

### Understanding

The unlock is that **Day 20 stops the last manual step without adding a single new faculty** — everything it needs already exists, and the day is mostly *naming* what the series has done since Day 7. The re-readings hit hardest in order: **(1) "Stop judging manually" is the month's whole Rule 4 finally given a machine** — the phrase "it seems better" (Rule 4's banned phrase) was never expelled by metrics, only demoted to a nicer-looking table; a genuine end to it requires the *grade itself* to be re-runnable, which determinism gives and hand-reading never will. **(2) The golden dataset is not new; it is Day 7's `PENDING` column promoted** — Day 7's notes said it outright: "the `PENDING` column is, almost exactly, Day 20's evaluation dataset (question, expected evidence, expected answer, failure category)." Nine questions that hand-built, what Day 20 systematizes; the field that was hard is hard for a reason, and the reason is the oracle seam — the expected answer must be *derivable from the expected evidence* or it is a hope, and `oracle.py` makes that checkable offline. **(3) The judge's honest shape is the trace, not the model** — every number the series trusts already lives in `trace.py`; a judge that re-answers would double the bill, bake noise into a "grade," and judge a run nobody gave, which is why "grade the trace, never re-answer" is the day's engineering spine. **(4) LLM-as-judge fails the way every model fails — and the failure is the argument, not the feature request** — a judge that drifts cannot fund a benchmark, a judge that admires fluency is rating prose, a judge that can be poisoned is a liability, and a judge that costs a chat per cell is spending where the free tier already decided; the honest lane is *off by default, explains but never decides*. And the day's own sobriety, mirroring every day since Day 14: **the entire court is free** — dataset YAML, judge arithmetic, regression assertions, all offline; the only thing that could cost real money is a semantic verdict, which is Day 21's purchase, not Day 20's.

### Implementation

- `ai_os/eval_dataset.py` (new) — the five-field schema + validator: `questions.yaml`/`code_questions.yaml` convert verbatim; expected answer is `claims[] + must_not_assert[]` derived from expected evidence via the oracle (offline; malformed → reject); expected tools checked against the Day 11 registry / Day 19 role table; `registered_at` + content-hash pinning.
- `ai_os/golden.yaml` (new) — the seed set: doc nine + code fixtures verbatim, plus the judge-hunting rows F/G/H and the E-family refusal rows, all five fields filled pre-run.
- `tools/eval_harness.py` (new) — the router: presents each golden question to the production path (stub/real, memory on-off, topology A/B), captures `{question, trace, ledger, config}` without touching answer-path code; refuses to grade under a stale hash (V1/V8).
- `ai_os/judge.py` (new) — the layered grader: layer 1 deterministic checks (`expected ⊆ retrieved`, `cited ⊆ retrieved`, `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, `tool == expected_tools`, `must_not`, refusal == expected, failure == category, cost, latency) → `verdict: pass|fail|pending` with owner per symptom; layer 2 semantic slots `PENDING`; layer 3 the flagged model lane behind `EVAL_JUDGE=deterministic|model|none`, writes only to `semantic:` and can never flip `deterministic_verdict`.
- `tools/regress_all.py` (new) — the Rule-2 gate: replays A1…A8, F1…F9, Q1…Q8, T1…T7, X1…X11, P1…P9, S1…S9, M1…M9, G1…G9 + doc/code golden rows via `fixtures.py`/`test_dayN.py`; report = "exactly as green, or the diff with owner."
- `docs/concepts/evaluation.md`, `docs/evaluations/eval-day20.md`.

### Failure

1. **The golden set becomes a dump.** Fifty happy-path questions, no E-family refusals, no judge-hunting rows — a set that flatters the system and cannot indict it. The anti-fix is V6's coverage rule and Rule 3 at the dataset level: every entry is a *kind of thing that has failed*, deliberately rebuilt; a set with no refusals is a set that will grade a hallucinator.
2. **The expected answer authored by hand.** Someone writes the model-voice paragraph the "right answer" should be — the seductive form of manual judgment, and the one the day's whole objective bans. The anti-fix is the claims-based form and the oracle-derivability check: `claims[]` is not a voice, and a claim the evidence can't reach is rejected at mint time.
3. **The curve fit.** `expected_evidence` edited after the run came up short; `expected_answer` typed after seeing the output. The anti-fix is the hash pin (V1/V8): pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting, and the harness refuses to grade a patched row.
4. **The judge re-answers.** The harness runs the model again to "check" the first run — doubling the bill, re-baking sampling noise, grading a run the trace never saw. The anti-fix is structural: the judge reads `trace.py` only; a judge that re-answers is a second production call that must not exist.
5. **The LLM judge placed in charge.** A model grader flips a pass to fail, and the `semantic:` column starts deciding. V4 is the guard: `deterministic_verdict` can only be set by layer 1; a graded semantic cell is `PENDING` by construction and owned by Day 21.
6. **The harness leaks into the answer path.** A judge in the loop, a golden row leaking into the corpus, a graded run running hotter than production — an evaluation that *improves* answers is an answer-path change wearing a lab coat (Rule 6 measured). The anti-fix is V9: installed and unused, the harness changes nothing, byte for byte.
7. **Regression forgotten in the glow of a golden "pass."** The golden set improves, the regress gate is skipped, and a Day-8 green goes red. The anti-fix is V5 and the split that cannot blur: improvement is measured on the golden set, *preservation* on the regression gate; one without the other is not a Rule-4 result.
8. **The judged-rows-are-the-known-rows trap.** Only the nine, forever — the golden set never grows, so the judge only re-verifies what it already knew. The anti-fix is the seed composition itself: the doc nine is the *baseline*, and judge-hunting F/G/H are the deliberate new enemies — a dataset that does not grow is a dataset that stops teaching.

### Debugging

Everything Day 20 adds is `MECH` under the stub: the golden set is YAML, the judge is membership, the regression gate is assertions, the hash is arithmetic. The debug loop is Day 7's, re-rooted onto the graders:

- **A verdict that cannot be reproduced** → the **judge** owns it — re-run the same trace and demand byte-identical rows (V2); a nondeterministic layer-1 check is a bug in the judge, not a property of answers.
- **A golden row that keeps: "failing"** → check the **entry first, not the pipeline** — is the expected answer oracle-derivable from its expected evidence (V1)? A malformed expectation is not a system failure; it is a golden-set failure.
- **A patched row passing** → the **hash pin** owns it — the pre-registration was violated; `assert hash == pinned_hash` is the line between a report and curve-fitting, and it fired.
- **A fluent-wrong answer passing** → the **judge's check set** owns it — `conclusion ⊆ evidence` (V3) should have caught the unsupported assertion; if it didn't, the check is missing the row's family, not the model.
- **An LLM-judge verdict that drifts** → the **V4 row** owns it — recorded, never trusted; the `semantic:` column is `PENDING` by definition and the deterministic verdict is the one shipped.
- **A prior-day fixture turning red** → the **regression gate** owns it (V5) — the diff names the row and Day 7's owner map names the layer; a red on the gate is a regression, never a "new expected behavior."
- **The golden run differing from a production run** → the **harness** owns it — V9's byte-identical trace is the contract; a reproduced trace is the only trace a verdict is allowed to grade.

The trace says which, never the vibes; the judge is deterministic by default; and each symptom has an owner from the day it was designed.

### Architecture

The claim of the day is the series' claim, one layer further: **the system learned to grade its own work, and it did it by making the oldest habit (pre-registering expectations) into a contract, not by building a grader.** No new engine exists — the golden set is Day 7's YAML with three more fields, the judge reads the trace the days already record, the regression gate replays the fixtures the days already wrote, and the honesty contract — `judged ⊆ pre-registered` — is Day 7's "pre-registration is the line between a report and curve-fitting" translated into an invariant, settable only by code. The judge is layered the way every day's honesty was layered: determinism first (`expected ⊆ retrieved`, `cited ⊆ retrieved`, `conclusion ⊆ evidence`, tool equality, refusal correctness — each an old invariant, now *issued by a machine*), semantics parked (`PENDING`, Day 21/22), and the model lane a flagged, budgeted, off-by-default comment that explains but never decides. The OFF switch (V9) proves the whole addition invisible: the judge is not a fixer, the final term in the line P9, S9, M8, G9 started — Rule 6's economy, applied to measurement itself. The seams are named and owned: RAG semantics → **Day 21**, agent behavior → **Day 22**, observability of the graded pipeline → **Day 23**, judge poisoning → **Day 25**, and the fully-golden prose answer → *named, not built* (the claims form is the deliberate replacement). **Day 7 pre-registered nine questions by hand, Days 8–19 improved against them by hand, Day 19 built the court for the architecture question — and Day 20 built the judge that all of them were waiting for, for $0. The series stopped judging AI quality manually without adding a single paid call; the machine that reads, searches, acts, plans, remembers, and decides now also grades — deterministically, honestly, and invisibly — so that Day 21 can take the stand and be graded against a real run.**

### Questions

- Day 21 owns the RAG benchmark — does "expected answer" as `claims[] + must_not_assert[]` expand, when graded by a real run, into a *claim-coverage* metric (what fraction of the mandatory claims appeared) rather than any holistic "answer correctness"? And is claim-coverage the honest bridge from Day 20's determinism to the semantic tier, or does it still smuggle a judge in through the back door?
- The oracle-derivability rule binds expected answers to expected evidence; but when the evidence *itself* is wrong (a doc that is genuinely erroneous), the golden set can only catch the citation, not the fact — is "the corpus can be wrong" an entry *class* Day 21 must add, or is it the boundary where evaluation must finally call the reader a human?
- A judge that must not re-answer is implicitly betting that the trace is complete — that no verdict-gradeable fact lives outside `trace.py`. Is Day 23's observability (whose whole point is capturing what the trace misses) the discovery procedure for exactly those missing facts, and should Day 21 re-run the golden set through the Day 23 trace as its first act?
- The model lane "explains but never decides" — but an explanation is still a judgment if anyone reads it. Should Day 21's semantic grader be a *see-judge-agree/see-judge-disagree* protocol against the deterministic cell, or is any human survivorship of the "semantic" verdict a regression to the manual grading Day 20 banned?
- V9 forbids the harness changing the answer — but the *golden set* explicitly changes what the system is trained/optimized against (the same questions get fixed, the same failures get re-litigated). When does regression-hardening the golden set become rule-fitting against a known battleship, and is a *rotating* held-out slice (a day-30 concern, named not built) the only honest answer, exactly as it is in classical eval?
